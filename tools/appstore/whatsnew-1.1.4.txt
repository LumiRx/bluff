Your first hand is now a hand you can play. The table deals you in as a caller — a clue, a keyboard, letters — and you set the word on hand two, once you know what a clue is worth.

The table is paper now. The word cards are cards: a printed rule, a stamp, a folded corner you could pick up. Stars move as markers you can count instead of a number that jumps. The keyboard is paper and ink.

And the first screen is one line and one button.
